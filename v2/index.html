Why Meddy

Care in India is split in pieces.

The crisis starts with fragmented custody of care: a patient's story is scattered across hospitals and clinics that don't talk to each other. It deepens as people lose autonomy over their own health: what they know, their history, and the options open to them.

2 minaverage primary-care consultation in IndiaBMJ Open, 2017
78%of doctors surveyed reported high burnout in the past yearSurvey of 1,208 doctors, 2025

Behind every rushed visit is a doctor.

Queue after queue, then calls, paperwork and follow-ups that no one else is set up to handle. The problem isn't the people. It's that clinics and hospitals still run on registers, memory and goodwill, without tools that carry part of the load.

So we work at both ends.

For clinics and hospitals, Meddy takes on the running of the practice, so doctors get their time back. For patients and caretakers, it keeps their care, history and options within reach.

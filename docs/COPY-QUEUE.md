**Links**

- 73%: https://www.ipsos.com/en-in/india-ranks-3-globally-ipsos-quality-care-perception-index-reflecting-positive-views-trust-and
- ~1 in 2: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41371-022-00754-1 (Kannure et al.: 51.3% followed up at least once, so about 48.7% lost)

**Design:** if three stats crowd the card, drop the 36.8% stat into the "Understanding" area of the patient page instead, and keep 73% plus ~1 in 2 together. The pair makes the point on its own: easy to start, hard to continue.

**Do not use** these other figures from the same Ipsos coverage:

- "70% self-medicate". Ipsos asked whether *people often* rely on OTC medicines or home remedies. That's a perception about others, not 70% saying they do it.
- "Indians most likely to trust AI chatbots". This isn't in the Ipsos health report. The Ipsos AI Monitor India uses an affluent online sample of 1,000 and gives no global rank.

**Caveat:** Ipsos India samples are online and lean urban and affluent. The 73% figure is Ipsos's own published claim, so citing it as "Ipsos" is accurate. Don't restate it as "73% of all Indians".

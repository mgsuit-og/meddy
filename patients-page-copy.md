## 2. The question

> **When did you last see your own complete medical record?**

For most of us, the answer is never. It's spread across hospitals, clinics, pharmacies and WhatsApp chats, and the job of joining it up falls on you.

---

## 3. Three problems

### 01 · Understanding
**You nodded. But did you understand?**
"Adenotonsillar hypertrophy." "Reflux oesophagitis." Medical words are hard for anyone to follow in a two-minute consultation. When the diagnosis or prescription isn't clear, it's harder to follow the treatment, and easier to make a mistake.

### 02 · Scattered records
**Five hospitals. Twelve doctors. No single record.**
Every new doctor starts from scratch. Old prescriptions get lost, and medicines are remembered by colour or shape instead of name. Carrying your history becomes your job.

### 03 · Preventable errors
**Most errors start with missing information.**
A medicine you forgot to mention. An allergy that isn't on file. In one Indian teaching-hospital study, **1 in 3 patients** had at least one prescribing error, and **two in three of those errors** involved medicines interacting with each other.
_Source: [Medication prescribing errors in a public teaching hospital in India, 2007](https://scielo.isciii.es/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S1885-642X2007000100003)_

## 6. Your right to your records

**Eyebrow:** Know your rights

### Your medical records belong to you.

Under India's medical ethics regulations, doctors must give you a copy of your medical records within **72 hours** of your request. Most people never ask. You can.

**[CHECK]** This comes from the Indian Medical Council (Professional Conduct, Etiquette and Ethics) Regulations, 2002, clause 1.3.2. As far as I know, it still applies because the NMC's 2023 replacement regulations were put on hold. Please have a lawyer confirm it before publishing.
